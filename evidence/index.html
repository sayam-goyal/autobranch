The third and fourth tiles are the point of showing this at all: a framework that only reports the runs it won is advertising, not evidence.

Finding 01 · Rocket League 1v1

One reward weight separates 0.922 from a policy that collapses

Ten branches, same environment, same trainer, same objective — only the reward differs. The spread is not marginal: the sparse control never leaves 0.355 in 401M steps, while the winner reaches 0.922. And at approach_ball = 20 the policy peaks at 0.599 and then falls to 0.353 — while its training return rose twentyfold.

Why this is the strongest chart here: the collapsing branch is the case hand-tuning cannot catch. Its training return was rising the whole time. Only an objective the reward cannot touch reports the fall — which is the one structural claim this framework makes.

Finding 02 · Street Fighter III, level-8 CPU

The search beat the hand-tuned reward by 22 points — and random beat them both

At matched model, interface and configuration, the searched reward (r3_potential) scored 77.0% against the hand-tuned reward's 55.0%. That is the comparison this framework exists to win, and it won it.

It is also not the whole picture. Uniform random over the same 40-macro vocabulary scores 87.5% and beats every trained policy by a significant margin.

What this actually shows: the reward search is doing real work and the environment's metric is compromised — a 40-macro action space where random play wins is measuring something other than skill. The honest reading is "the search improves the reward; this benchmark cannot certify the result." Reporting only the 77 vs 55 bars would have hidden that.

Finding 03 · Tetris Battle

The best training signal in the sweep was worth exactly nothing

Six arms, each scored two ways: by self-play win rate (what training sees) and by an independent greedy opponent (what counts). Self-play ranges from 0.372 to 0.807. The objective is 0.000 for every single one.

This is why the objective is structurally separated. The evaluator imports no reward module, enforced by a test that walks the import graph. Without that wall, the arm at 0.807 self-play looks like progress and ships.

Finding 04 · Tetris Battle

Sometimes the answer is "this axis is flat" — and that is worth $4.51

Two independent reward rewrites and a shaping scale swept from 0.0 to 4.0 produced one number: 0.000. Then the policy's representation changed — scoring the board each placement would produce, instead of choosing among 240 abstract action indices — and the same reward, byte-identical, scored 0.994.

Against the framework, honestly: autobranch branches mutate rewards. It cannot make the change that mattered here — that is a different network, not a different weight. What it can do is rule out the axis it does search in six branches and $4.51, which is the useful half of this result.

Finding 05 · Rocket League, cost

The winner cost $6.25. The other $23.65 bought knowing why

Per-branch spend against the best score each reached. The top-right point is the winner. The point above it scored 0.930 — higher — and was still abandoned, because it needed 1,325M steps to reach what the winner reached in 1,140M.

The efficiency claim, stated narrowly: a branch forks from a filesystem snapshot, so exploring six rewards from a checkpoint costs six continuations rather than six runs from scratch. That is why ten branches fit in $29.90 — not because any single branch is cheap.

Limits

What these charts do not show

Three of four runs are reconstructions. Rocket League and Tetris map hand-driven sweeps onto the branch tree the framework would have produced; every score, weight and step count is real, but the agent turns are not. Each run's PROVENANCE block says which parts are measured and which are reconstructed.

The live SF3 run has not finished. Its one score — 0.750 at n = 24, interval [0.551, 0.880] — is 33 points wide and was measured with Hinh's reward, not a searched one. It is the search's baseline, not its product.

No head-to-head against a published model exists. "Beats Hinh" here means beats his reward reimplemented in our harness, at matched size and configuration. His checkpoint has never been run against ours on the same opponent.

And the framework is not always the right tool. Finding 04 is a case where the entire axis it searches was flat, and the improvement came from somewhere it cannot reach.
