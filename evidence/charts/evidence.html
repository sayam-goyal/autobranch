What the measurements actually support

autobranch searches reward functions with a tree of agents in Modal sandboxes. Every number below came from an evaluator that imports no reward code — including the one case where the search found nothing at all.

4 environments30+ branches objective-only evidenceintervals, not point estimates

Exhibit 1

The reward has a window, with an edge on both sides

Ten branches of a Rocket League search, each a different reward, all scored by the same evaluator. The spread is not noise — it is the entire outcome.

Exhibit 2

Training signal does not predict the objective

Each point is one Tetris branch: how well it was doing against its own self-play opponents, against how well it actually played.

Exhibit 3

Where the search found nothing — and what that cost

Twenty branches across two projects, plotted as what each one cost against the best it ever scored. Circles are Rocket League, squares Tetris.

Exhibit 4

Searched reward against hand-tuned, same harness

Street Fighter III, win rate against the level-8 CPU. The first two bars are matched on model, size and configuration — the reward is the only difference between them.

Exhibit 5

The run in flight, on one set of axes

Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, full-parameter fine-tune, eleven iterations. Mean return and policy entropy are what the trainer reports every iteration; the single blue point is the only thing that counts.

Sources: autobranch/seed_runs.py and seed_runs_tetris.py carry the Rocket League and Tetris measurements with a PROVENANCE block naming which parts are measured and which reconstructed; the Street Fighter bars come from SF_REPORT.md (n=100 each) and the live run's own metrics.jsonl on the Modal volume. Intervals are Wilson 95%. Chart coordinates are computed from those files rather than placed by hand, so a point cannot disagree with its own caption.
